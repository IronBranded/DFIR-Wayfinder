Finishing this academy answers "can I do the work." A certification answers "can I prove it to someone who's never seen my work" — a different, complementary question, and worth knowing which GIAC certifications map to what you've actually just spent eight levels learning.

> [!PLAIN]
> GIAC (Global Information Assurance Certification) is SANS's certification body — when this academy cites "SANS FOR508" or "SANS FOR572" as a source, GIAC is the certification that specific course leads toward. They're proctored, timed, hands-on-graded exams, not multiple-choice trivia.

## The three that map directly onto this academy

**GCFE — GIAC Certified Forensic Examiner.** The foundational Windows-forensics certification: evidence acquisition, browser history, user activity artifacts, e-discovery. This maps almost directly onto **Level 2 (Windows Endpoint Forensics)** and the artifact-reading discipline that runs through this entire academy. If you're going to sit one exam first, this is usually it.

**GCFA — GIAC Certified Forensic Analyst.** The advanced step up from GCFE: formal incident investigation, memory forensics, timeline analysis, and anti-forensics detection — both Windows and Linux. This is the certification tied to SANS FOR508, and it maps onto **Level 5 (Memory Forensics)** plus the anti-forensics content in **Level 7**, built on top of the Level 2 foundation.

**GCIH — GIAC Certified Incident Handler.** Broader than either forensics certification — the full incident-handling lifecycle: detection, containment, eradication, recovery. This is the certification that maps most directly onto **Level 1's IR Lifecycle lesson** and the **playbooks in Level 8** — less about reading one artifact deeply, more about running the whole response correctly.

## Two more, if your work points that direction

**GNFA — GIAC Network Forensic Analyst.** Tied to SANS FOR572, and the certification that would extend what **Level 7's Network Forensics content** deliberately stops short of — this academy covers log- and flow-level network evidence on purpose, not full packet capture, and GNFA is where that fuller discipline lives if your role needs it.

**GCTI — GIAC Cyber Threat Intelligence.** If **Level 1's Diamond Model and threat-intelligence-operationalization lessons** were the parts that clicked hardest for you, this is the certification built around exactly that kind of adversary-profiling work rather than single-incident analysis.

## A realistic order, not a required one

There's no rule that says you need all five, or any of them. But if you're planning a path: **GCFE → GCFA** is the standard forensics progression (GCFA generally assumes GCFE-level material as a starting point), and **GCIH** is commonly taken alongside or before either, since incident-handling fundamentals underpin both. GNFA and GCTI are specializations worth adding once you know which direction your actual work is pulling you — not something to front-load before you've found that out.

None of this requires waiting until you've finished every lesson in this academy first. The overlap above is deliberate: by the time Level 2 and Level 5 are both marked complete, you already have the GCFE/GCFA study foundation, certification or no certification.
